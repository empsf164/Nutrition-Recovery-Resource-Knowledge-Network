How Recovery Nutrition Works: Cellular Repair & Glycogen Resynthesis

In this visual lecture, Dr. Elena Rostova breaks down the enzymatic pathways governing glycogen storage, amino acid transporters, and practical post-exercise nutrient combinations that minimize metabolic fatigue.

Dr. Elena Rostova, PhD Senior Fellow in Exercise Physiology

Interactive Transcript

00:00

"Welcome to NOURIVA's masterclass on recovery biochemistry. Today we are going to explore what actually occurs inside the myocyte during the first 120 minutes following strenuous muscular effort."

02:15

"Glycogen depletion is not uniform. Type II fast-twitch fibers deplete glucose polymers significantly faster, demanding high-glycemic or easily transportable monosaccharides immediately post-session."

05:40

"When we examine the 3:1 carbohydrate-to-protein ratio, we observe a synergistic insulin release that upregulates GLUT4 translocation without blunting cellular fatty acid oxidation excessively."

09:20

"Leucine is not just a building block; it acts as a molecular light switch. Reaching ~2.7g of free leucine in plasma triggers mTORC1 phosphorylation and turns on ribosome protein synthesis."

12:10

"Practical kitchen application: combining sweet potatoes with wild salmon or oats with a clean pea-rice blend provides both the requisite glycemic curve and complete essential amino acid profile."

13:40

"To summarize: hydrate with 1.25x the fluid lost, hit your leucine threshold within 90 minutes, and ensure high sleep quality to allow growth hormone to complete cellular repair."
