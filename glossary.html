Nutrition & Recovery Glossary

Clear, peer-reviewed definitions of essential terminology across human biochemistry, exercise recovery, and dietary science.

A
Amino Acids (Essential vs Non-Essential)
Biochemistry

Organic compounds containing amine and carboxyl functional groups. Nine "essential" amino acids cannot be synthesized endogenously by the human body and must be acquired via dietary protein.

Related: Protein & Leucine Guide
Anthocyanins
Phytonutrients

Water-soluble flavonoid pigments found in dark berries, tart cherries, and purple potatoes that possess strong antioxidant and exercise-induced inflammation modulating properties.

B
Bioavailability
Dietary Science

The fraction of an administered or ingested nutrient that reaches systemic circulation in an active state capable of eliciting a physiological effect.

Branched-Chain Amino Acids (BCAAs)
Biochemistry

A subgroup of essential amino acids (Leucine, Isoleucine, Valine) characterized by a non-linear aliphatic side chain, oxidized directly within skeletal muscle tissue.

C
Carbohydrate-to-Protein Ratio (3:1 / 4:1)
Recovery Protocol

The optimal macronutrient ratio consumed within the initial post-exercise window to maximize glycogen resynthesis kinetics via insulin-mediated glucose transport.

E
Electrolyte Osmolality
Hydration

The concentration of solute particles per kilogram of solvent. Isotonic and slightly hypotonic beverages (200–290 mOsm/kg) promote the most rapid fluid absorption across intestinal membranes.

G
Glycogen Synthase
Enzymatic Recovery

The primary regulatory enzyme responsible for converting UDP-glucose into stored multi-branched glycogen polymers within liver and skeletal muscle cells.

L
Leucine Threshold
Protein Science

The minimum intracellular concentration of leucine (~2.5–3.0 grams per meal) required to fully activate the mTORC1 signaling cascade and initiate muscle protein synthesis.

M
Muscle Protein Synthesis (MPS)
Cellular Repair

The fundamental biochemical process wherein cells construct new myofibrillar proteins to repair microscopic damage and adapt to muscular exertion.

O
Osmotic Gradient
Hydration

The pressure differential between intracellular and extracellular fluid compartments driving water transport across semipermeable cell membranes.

P
Protein Digestibility-Corrected Amino Acid Score (PDCAAS)
Dietary Metric

A method of evaluating protein quality based on both the amino acid requirements of humans and their ability to digest it.
